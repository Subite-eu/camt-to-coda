# North America: bank-by-bank CAMT reporting review

Checked **11 October 2026**. This bounded primary-source review covers **38 bank/jurisdiction checks: 8 Canada, 23 United States or US-account/global-bank checks, and 7 Mexico**. Research depth varies: some banks publish detailed technical guides, while others only expose product pages or region-specific leads. It is not a census of every North American institution. Caribbean and Central American banks are not comprehensively inventoried.

This report separates bank reporting services from payment-network ISO 20022 adoption. **Not publicly verified means insufficient public evidence, not unsupported.** Contracted services, exact account eligibility and newer rollout dates need confirmation with the bank. A global bank source does not establish every local branch’s capabilities. Pages in a country’s language do not prove account jurisdiction.

Use **Bank catalog** in the app for official resources and country filters. The North America filter links here, including outcomes that cannot responsibly be listed as live bank offerings. Global-only BofA/Citi evidence remains in the catalog’s **Unspecified global markets** group; it is reviewed here without inventing US-booked account eligibility. The [canonical catalog](catalog.json) supplies the maintained positive entries; this dated review records additional uncertainties and historical findings.

## Verification rules

Positive claims use bank-primary publications and state channel, customer and jurisdiction limits. Publication dates are retained where available; the check date is not a document publication date. Schema suffixes are recorded only when explicitly published or previously verified from unchanged originals. Generic ISO migration, payment-message support, incoming message acceptance, UI language and unverified SWIFT profile labels do not establish outbound corporate CAMT reporting.

Catalog generation checks identifiers, HTTPS source links, resource/access labels and unchanged fixture linkage. Browser regression tests cover country separation, explicit FI scope, discoverable uncertainty notes and official example/specification links. These tests verify the application’s presentation and metadata consistency; they cannot independently certify a bank’s private production service.
